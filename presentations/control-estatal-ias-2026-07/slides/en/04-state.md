<!--
VOICE:
"Why does the state get involved? The honest reason isn't a metaphor, it's
two AI-safety ideas.

Dual use: the same capability that finds vulnerabilities to defend you finds
them to attack you. There's no clean line between good and bad use. Glasswing
is exactly that: giving the offensive model to defenders, betting they win
the race.

And the asymmetry: attacking is often easier than defending. As the
literature puts it, 'an attacker only needs to find one vulnerability; the
defenders must protect and fix all of them.' The attacker needs to be right
once; the defender, every time.

[CLICK]

Put the two together and you have the real, non-paranoid reason a state feels
it must control who has the capability. The asymmetry is genuine. But a
legitimate worry doesn't make any control legitimate. The question isn't
whether to control, but by what rules. And that's the final block."

NOTES:
